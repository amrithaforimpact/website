Reaching them 20 years sooner

Youth4Jobs runs community training centers for young adults with disabilities from low-income families. Many arrive in their thirties or forties, having left school decades earlier, after years of discovering that the world wasn't designed for them. The centers train them and place them in jobs. It works. But it comes 20 years too late.

So we asked a different question: what if we reached them while they were still in school?

I was the first hire on that pilot, and I hired and led the team of 10 who ran it with me. We began with more than 200 deaf and hard of hearing students in public schools serving low-income communities. Four things had to change at once.

The classroom. I built the assistive-technology model at our pilot school and trained teachers to use it, so students could learn in a way that worked for them.

The instruction. We hired instructors fluent in Indian Sign Language, so students were taught in their own language.

The system. Scaling required the state's approval. I mapped five decision-makers, worked through three rounds of proposals, and rebuilt the path when our sponsoring official was transferred. The government authorized the program in public schools, and more than 150 schools opened their doors to assistive technology.

The partners. Deloitte funded the program and helped shape its design. As it expanded statewide, I worked with Deloitte to direct its software donations: I set the criteria for which schools took part, trained their teachers, and reported results back to Deloitte.

The pathway. Students moved from school into Youth4Jobs' training centers for advanced technology training, and from there into jobs. I built hiring pipelines with employers including Amazon, HSBC and PepsiCo. What once took two decades became one continuous path.

What it taught me
  • The earlier the investment, the greater the return. Reaching people upstream changes the whole arc of their lives, not just the next step.
  • Capacity outlasts the donation. Donated technology builds lasting capacity when it follows a model co-designed with schools, not when it's dropped into them.
  • Proof moves policy. A pilot done well can become systems change, reshaping what an entire state funds.
