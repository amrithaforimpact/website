When the rules changed mid-year, the funding doubled

At the International Rescue Committee, I helped run workforce and housing programs for refugee, asylee and unhoused families in San Jose, California, in partnership with the city. Families came to us after displacement from Afghanistan, Ukraine, Syria, El Salvador and beyond, often with skills and credentials that didn't yet count in a new country.

Our work covered the full path to stability: job readiness, placement with employers, housing, and help navigating benefits and public systems. Over two years, we supported more than 1,200 households. When referrals surged during the Afghan evacuation, I built a simple triage process that let our team enroll more than 100 clients a week.

Then, midway through the year, a state policy change tightened eligibility. Many families we had planned to serve no longer qualified, and our contract targets were suddenly out of reach.

Rather than wait for the quarter to close and report a shortfall, we moved early. We redirected outreach toward households that still qualified. I went to our funders with what had changed, what our first year had already achieved, and revised targets we could stand behind.

They didn't just stay with us. Program funding doubled, from roughly $250,000 to $500,000.

What it taught me
  • Trust is built before the crisis. Funders back partners who come to them early with a plan, not just a problem.
  • Evidence is leverage. A clear record of outcomes can turn a setback into a case for greater investment.
  • Adapt before the targets slip. When the ground shifts, the strategy has to shift first.
