How I work

A few things I do on every project.

First, I go where the work is.
Before I plan anything, I sit with the people it's for, in classrooms, homes and community centers. My best ideas have come from those conversations, never from a desk.

Second, I look for the one thing that's really in the way.
Once, it was instructors who weren't fluent in sign language. Another time, it was an intake line out the door during a refugee surge. Fix that one thing, and the rest starts to move.

Third, I tell partners early, especially when the news isn't good.
When a policy change shrank the number of families we could serve, I went to our funders before the quarter closed. Our funding doubled instead of shrinking.

Fourth, I speak the listener's language.
A government official, a CSR leader and a mother each need to hear why it matters to them. I keep it simple, and I listen more than I talk.

People often tell me I'm easy to talk to. I take that as the highest compliment, because nothing good in this work happens without trust.
