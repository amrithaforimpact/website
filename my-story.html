About me

From code to classrooms to communities.

I build programs that open doors for people who've been kept outside them.

In college, I volunteered in a classroom where a teacher barely older than me was everything to their students: teacher, mentor and guide. I couldn't stop thinking about it.

I still started out writing code at Cognizant. But most evenings I left asking myself what I'd actually done that day. A couple of years later, after a detour into digital marketing in Mumbai, I was a Teach For India fellow, teaching 400 students in a public school in India. That classroom set the course for everything after.

Teaching showed me what teachers carry and what parents hope for. I took that behind the screen, shaping an ed-tech platform built around how children actually learn and what teachers and families need. It reached more than 35,000 young learners across five school districts.

Much of my work since has been about economic mobility: the path from learning to earning. I built a skilling and placement program for young people with disabilities, from a single pilot to a state government order, placing young people with employers like Amazon, HSBC and PepsiCo. In San Jose, California, I helped refugee families rebuild after displacement from Afghanistan, Ukraine, Syria, El Salvador and beyond. That was more than 1,200 households, and thousands of family members, moving into steady work and stable homes, the first step toward higher lifetime earnings.

My work has crossed languages, cultures and continents. I've made the case to state officials, CSR leaders and hiring managers, and sat with families starting over in a new country. Wherever I am, I can explain why a program matters to the people funding it and the people it serves.

The sectors have changed. The thread hasn't. And I'm just getting started.
